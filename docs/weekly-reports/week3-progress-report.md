Week 3 Progress Report: Context Engineering and RAG
SACCOMember-CasePreparationAgent
Reporting period: 14–18 September 2026
Week 3 focused on implementing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) so that the SACCO Member-Case Preparation Agent can answer questions from approved, controlled and traceable policy documents instead of relying only on the language model’s general knowledge. This was important because SACCO loan decisions involve sensitive information such as member eligibility, savings requirements, repayment periods, interest rates, required documents, guarantors, approval limits and repayment procedures. The system must therefore provide answers that can be verified against the SACCO’s approved policies.
During the week, a controlled knowledge corpus was defined for the project. The proposed corpus includes the SACCO bylaws, credit policy and lending procedures manual, savings policy, loan-product guidelines, credit-risk policy, accounting procedures manual, governance and committee terms of reference, AML/KYC policy, consumer-protection policy, data-protection policy, loan application forms, appraisal checklists, guarantor forms, repayment schedule templates, synthetic member profiles and synthetic case packs. Each document was assigned a source record containing its title, document type, owner, origin, version, approval date, effective date, access status and relevance to the member-case preparation agent. This source register will help the system distinguish between current approved policies, archived policies, draft documents and supporting reference materials.
A key decision was made to use vector-based RAG for the first implementation. This approach is suitable because most of the SACCO knowledge is contained in policy documents, procedures, forms and manuals. The documents are divided into meaningful sections and converted into searchable representations. Each section is stored together with metadata such as the document title, page number, section heading, policy version, approval status and effective date. When a user asks a question, the system searches the indexed content and retrieves the sections that are most relevant to the question. The retrieved evidence is then supplied to the language model as context before an answer is generated.
The system was designed to display the sources used in each response. For example, if a loan officer asks which documents are required for a development loan, the agent should retrieve the relevant section from the credit policy or loan-product guideline and show the document title, policy version, section and page where available. This improves transparency and allows the loan officer, Credit Committee or internal auditor to verify the answer. Source traceability is particularly important for the SACCO because a response without evidence may be difficult to trust or audit.
The RAG prompt was also designed to restrict the model to the retrieved evidence. The model is instructed not to invent policy rules, repayment periods, interest rates or eligibility requirements. Where the available evidence is incomplete, the model must clearly state that the information is insufficient and refer the matter to a loan officer or Credit Committee. This prevents the system from giving confident but unsupported answers. The system also separates policy interpretation from financial computation. The retrieved policy can provide the interest method, loan period and repayment conditions, but a deterministic calculation engine should calculate principal, interest, installments, penalties and due dates.
A fifteen-question RAG evaluation set was prepared to test the performance of the retrieval and grounding process. The questions include answerable, partially answerable and deliberately unanswerable cases. Answerable questions relate to information clearly contained in the corpus, such as required loan documents, maximum repayment periods and guarantor requirements. Partially answerable questions require information that may be missing, such as determining whether a member qualifies when the member’s current savings balance or existing loan balance has not been provided. Deliberately unanswerable questions test whether the system refuses to guess when a product, policy rule or approval limit is not present in the corpus. The evaluation records the question, expected response, retrieved source, actual response, citation quality and pass or fail result.
Three main retrieval problems were identified. The first problem was incomplete chunking, where a policy rule and its exception could be separated into different sections, causing the model to receive only part of the rule. This will be mitigated by using structure-aware segmentation, preserving headings, keeping tables together and adding controlled overlap between related sections.     The third problem was unsupported model assumptions. The model might retrieve a relevant paragraph but add information that is not stated in the source, such as assuming that a member is eligible simply because the member has savings. This will be controlled through evidence-only instructions, mandatory source references, insufficient-evidence responses and human review of recommendations.
Other challenges included differences in formatting between PDF and Word documents, duplicate rules appearing in more than one policy, unclear policy wording and the possibility of mixing internal SACCO procedures with general regulatory information. These challenges were addressed by adding document metadata, assigning priority to approved internal policies, separating internal sources from external laws and guidance, and requiring a staff member to review documents before they are added to the controlled corpus.
The main achievements for Week 3 were the definition of the controlled corpus, creation of the source register, design of the RAG architecture, definition of the ingestion and retrieval process, preparation of the fifteen-question evaluation set and documentation of common retrieval and grounding failures. The project also established important engineering boundaries by ensuring that the language model explains retrieved policy information while deterministic software performs financial calculations. This improves reliability, consistency and auditability.
The main expectation for the next stage is to connect the RAG pipeline to safe application tools. The following week will focus on function calling, including a tool for retrieving synthetic member-case data and a low-risk tool for creating a draft case note. These tools will use defined input and output schemas, authorization checks, error handling and audit logging. Human approval will be required before any higher-impact action, such as submitting a case to the Credit Committee, approving a loan or changing member financial information.
