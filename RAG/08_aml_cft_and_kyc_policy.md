# Anti-Money Laundering, Combating Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) & KYC Compliance Policy

## 1. Regulatory Context and Legal Mandate
* **1.1 Statutory Basis:** Enacted pursuant to the Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2013 (as amended 2017), the Anti-Terrorism Act, 2002 (as amended), and guidelines issued by the Financial Intelligence Authority (FIA) Uganda and UMRA.
* **1.2 Institutional Zero-Tolerance:** The SACCO enforces a zero-tolerance stance towards the laundering of illicit proceeds, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing.

## 2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and KYC Tiers
* **2.1 Standard Customer Due Diligence (Level 1 KYC - Account Opening):**
  1. Full legal name verified against Ugandan National Identity Card (NIN) issued by NIRA.
  2. Verified physical residential address corroborated by Local Council 1 (LC1) Chairperson letter.
  3. Contact telephone number registered under the applicant's verified NIN.
  4. Recent passport-sized color photograph.
* **2.2 Enhanced Due Diligence (Level 2 KYC - Credit Applicants & High Value):**
  1. Mandatory verification of business premises or agricultural land via on-site visit by SACCO staff.
  2. Documented proof of legitimate source of funds for repayments (pay-slips, audited books, tax TIN certificate, certified bank statements).
  3. Verification of beneficial ownership for group or corporate SACCO members.

## 3. Screening for Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
* **3.1 PEP Definition:** Any individual who is or has been entrusted with prominent public functions in Uganda or abroad (e.g., Members of Parliament, Ministers, Judges, Senior Military Officers, Local Government Chairpersons LC5, Senior Parastatal Executives), including immediate family members and close associates.
* **3.2 EDD for PEPs:**
  * PEP loan applications cannot be approved under standard branch thresholds.
  * Senior Management and Full Board review is mandatory for all PEP credit relationships.
  * Source of wealth and source of funds must be corroborated by documentary proof prior to file appraisal.

## 4. Large Cash Transaction Reporting (CTR)
* **4.1 Mandatory Threshold:** In accordance with FIA regulations, any single cash deposit, withdrawal, or loan payout equal to or exceeding **UGX 20,000,000** (or its equivalent in foreign currency) must be reported to the FIA via a Large Cash Transaction Report (CTR) within five (5) business days.
* **4.2 Smurfing / Structuring Prohibition:** Deliberate splitting of transactions into smaller amounts beneath the threshold to evade reporting is strictly prohibited and constitutes grounds for an immediate Suspicious Transaction Report (STR).

## 5. Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR) Protocols
* **5.1 Red Flags for Credit Operations:**
  * Sudden repayment of a long-term loan using large amounts of cash from an unknown source without economic justification.
  * Reluctance to disclose physical address or provide verifiable National ID.
  * Borrower requesting loan disbursement to a third party unrelated to the documented transaction.
* **5.2 Tipping-Off Prohibition:** Staff must never disclose to the applicant, member, or external parties that a file or transaction has been flagged or reported to the FIA. Tipping-off is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment under Ugandan law.
